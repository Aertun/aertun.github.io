A proof of this kind is an in-toto Statement v1 in a DSSE envelope whose predicateType is this page's address. Every kind is described on Proofs.

What it claims

No indicator in a vendor's disclosure of its own breach was seen in this organisation's events outside the window the vendor gave, nor inside it when the vendor said this organisation was not affected.

Its verdict

refuted when an indicator was seen before or after the window, or inside it when the vendor said this organisation was not affected, each named with how many events carried it; proven when nothing contradicts the disclosure and the store holds the whole of the window and of the margin either side, so an indicator not found was not there; inconclusive otherwise, naming the period the store does not hold. The disclosure’s title is never read and its indicators are carried only in canonical form.

aertun vendor check-breach acme-2026-07.json --margin 720h --key signing.key --out acme.proof.json

What every proof carries

FieldHolds

The evidence: predicate.breach_scope

BreachScopeEvidence is what a breach-scope proof rests on.

Generated from the program's own definition of this predicate, so these are the names a proof carries.

Verifying one

aertun proof verify --key proof-key.json PROOF.json                 # both signatures, and the verdict
python3 scripts/verifyproof/verify.py PROOF.json proof-key.json     # without Aertun

Both signatures are over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding of the payload: Ed25519 as DSSE defines it, and ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) in the context aertun/proof/v1. A signature says which key signed, never whose; whether to trust the key is yours to decide, and a store's trusted signers say whose it is there. Nothing an author wrote is copied into a proof beyond identifiers and numbers that passed a strict parser.
