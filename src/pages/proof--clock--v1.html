A proof of this kind is an in-toto Statement v1 in a DSSE envelope whose predicateType is this page's address. Every kind is described on Proofs.

What it claims

An incident's decision — aware, major, material, personal, health or mitigated — was recorded by a verified person in an entry of Aertun's signed audit log, the incident's own record agrees with it, the entry is under a log head anchored in Bitcoin, and every obligation the decision started was discharged by its deadline.

Its verdict

refuted when the log has no entry for the decision, the incident’s record of it or of a discharge disagrees with the log, an anchored log head no longer matches the log, or an obligation was discharged late or is past due, each named; proven when none of that, the decision was made by a verified person, a confirmed Bitcoin attestation covers its entry, and every obligation is discharged or ruled out; inconclusive otherwise: no anchor yet, an unverified person, or a clock still running. The deadlines are the published headline ones; the proof is arithmetic on clocks people started, not legal advice.

aertun incident prove inc-20261110-abc123 --trigger aware --key signing.key --out aware.proof.json

What every proof carries

FieldHolds
verdictproven, refuted or inconclusive
claimthe sentence that was checked, in words
authorwho made the work under test, where that is known: {"by","model","prompt_sha256"} — a digest of the prompt, never the prompt
verifierwhat checked it: {"id","version","program"}
materialsevery input the verifier read besides the subject, each as {"name","digest","entries"}
checkseach check as {"name","passed","detail","failures"}: what it found, and each failure named
clockthe evidence below, which is set only on this kind
verified_atwhen the verifier ran, UTC, RFC 3339

The evidence: predicate.clock

ClockEvidence is what a clock proof rests on.

FieldTypeMeans
incidentstringIncident is the incident's identifier.
triggerstringTrigger is the decision: aware, major, material, personal, health or mitigated.
entryintEntry is the audit entry that recorded the decision; zero when the log has none.
decidedstringDecided is when the log recorded it, RFC 3339 to the nanosecond.
recordedstringRecorded is when the incident's own record says it was made.
verifiedboolVerified says the person who made it proved who they were.
anchor*ClockHead — {"size","root","block","calendar"}: a log head, the Bitcoin block its attestation is in, and the calendar’s hostAnchor is the earliest head covering the entry with a confirmed Bitcoin attestation that still matches the log.
broken[]ClockHead — {"size","root","block","calendar"}: a log head, the Bitcoin block its attestation is in, and the calendar’s hostBroken are anchored heads that no longer match the log.
linkedintLinked is how many findings were linked to the incident when the decision was made.
lead_secondsintLead is how long before the decision the earliest of them was first seen, in seconds.
duties[]ClockDuty — each as {"regime","due","done","waived","moved","recorded","outcome"}: an obligation by its name in the table, its deadline, when the log recorded it discharged, and met, late, open or waivedDuties are the obligations the decision started.

Generated from the program's own definition of this predicate, so these are the names a proof carries.

Verifying one

aertun proof verify --key proof-key.json PROOF.json                 # both signatures, and the verdict
python3 scripts/verifyproof/verify.py PROOF.json proof-key.json     # without Aertun

Both signatures are over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding of the payload: Ed25519 as DSSE defines it, and ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) in the context aertun/proof/v1. A signature says which key signed, never whose; whether to trust the key is yours to decide, and a store's trusted signers say whose it is there. Nothing an author wrote is copied into a proof beyond identifiers and numbers that passed a strict parser.
