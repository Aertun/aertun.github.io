A proof of this kind is an in-toto Statement v1 in a DSSE envelope whose predicateType is this page's address. Every kind is described on Proofs.

What it claims

For each ATT&CK, ATLAS or OWASP technique the rules claim, every corpus entry labelled as an instance of it — by what produced it, such as an Atomic Red Team test — is caught by a rule claiming it that raises an alert, no such rule fires on an entry labelled benign, and, with telemetry, every instance caught is still caught with only the fields its source has sent lately.

Its verdict

refuted when an instance is caught by no rule claiming its technique, or a claiming rule fires on a benign entry; proven when every technique has its instances caught on at least the evidence asked for; inconclusive when an instance is seen only by a rule that raises nothing on its own, the evidence is thin, or an instance would no longer be caught with the fields its source sends now. A sub-technique is an instance of its parent. There is no percentage; --navigator draws an ATT&CK Navigator layer of it.

aertun proof coverage --corpus corpus.jsonl --technique T1556 --telemetry 7d --key signing.key --navigator layer.json --out coverage.proof.json

What every proof carries

FieldHolds

Verifying one

aertun proof verify --key proof-key.json PROOF.json                 # both signatures, and the verdict
python3 scripts/verifyproof/verify.py PROOF.json proof-key.json     # without Aertun

Both signatures are over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding of the payload: Ed25519 as DSSE defines it, and ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) in the context aertun/proof/v1. A signature says which key signed, never whose; whether to trust the key is yours to decide, and a store's trusted signers say whose it is there. Nothing an author wrote is copied into a proof beyond identifiers and numbers that passed a strict parser.
