A proof of this kind is an in-toto Statement v1 in a DSSE envelope whose predicateType is this page's address. Every kind is described on Proofs.

What it claims

A managed detection provider, or an AI SOC, notified within its service level on all but a declared share of emulations run on the estate at random times, from a plan whose digest the audit log committed before any emulation ran.

Its verdict

refuted when the exact lower bound on the share missed or late is over the tolerance; proven when the upper bound is at or under it once the plan’s window has closed; inconclusive otherwise. Runs and notifications are recorded by verified people, or notifications joined from the provider’s alerts in the event store.

aertun mdr plan q4 --provider acme-mdr --emulations emulations.txt --count 20 --over 30d
aertun mdr prove q4 --sla 30m --tolerance 0.1 --key signing.key --out mdr.proof.json

What every proof carries

FieldHolds

The evidence: predicate.mdr

MDREvidence is what an MDR proof rests on.

FieldTypeMeans
planstringPlan is the plan's name, as the operator gave it.
providerstringProvider is the provider under test, by a short name the operator chose: never the provider's own wording.
plan_sha256stringPlanSHA256 is the digest of the plan as it was committed, which is also the subject's digest.
committedAuditEntryCommitted is the audit entry that committed the plan's digest, before any item was run.
window_startstringWindowStart and WindowEnd bound the times the items were drawn at, RFC 3339; the end is not included.
window_endstring
run_within_secondsintRunWithin is how long after its scheduled time an item could still be run and counted.
sla_secondsintSLA is the time to notify the provider is held to, in seconds.
closedboolClosed is whether the test had closed when the proof was made: the window's end, the time to run its last item, and the SLA after that, all passed.
tolerancefloat64Tolerance is the missed share the provider is held to, declared when the proof was made.
plannedintPlanned is how many items the plan holds.
runintRun is how many of them were recorded as run.
first_runstringFirstRun is when the first of them was run, RFC 3339 as recorded; empty when none was.
within_slaintWithin is how many runs the provider notified within the SLA.
lateintLate is how many it notified after the SLA.
not_notifiedintNever is how many it did not notify at all.
missedBound — k of n with exact one-sided bounds at 95% each: {"k","n","lower","upper"}Missed is the late and the never, of the runs, with the exact one-sided bounds on the share.
median_secondsintMedian is the time to notify, in seconds, of the runs notified late or in time: the nearest-rank 50th percentile, zero when none was.

Generated from the program's own definition of this predicate, so these are the names a proof carries.

Verifying one

aertun proof verify --key proof-key.json PROOF.json                 # both signatures, and the verdict
python3 scripts/verifyproof/verify.py PROOF.json proof-key.json     # without Aertun

Both signatures are over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding of the payload: Ed25519 as DSSE defines it, and ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) in the context aertun/proof/v1. A signature says which key signed, never whose; whether to trust the key is yours to decide, and a store's trusted signers say whose it is there. Nothing an author wrote is copied into a proof beyond identifiers and numbers that passed a strict parser.
