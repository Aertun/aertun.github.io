A proof of this kind is an in-toto Statement v1 in a DSSE envelope whose predicateType is this page's address. Every kind is described on Proofs.

What it claims

Every record of a peer deployment's audit log up to a time is held here in order, each tree head the peer signed as it sent them is the Merkle root of the records held, and the peer has signed no other history.

Its verdict

refuted when a signed head is not the records held, or the peer sent a head of another history signed with its own keys, which the proof carries; proven when every head agrees, there is no fork, and the last batch arrived after the time and short of a full batch; inconclusive otherwise.

aertun inbound prove site-b --key signing.key --at 2026-11-18T00:00:00Z --out site-b.proof.json

What every proof carries

FieldHolds
verdictproven, refuted or inconclusive
claimthe sentence that was checked, in words
authorwho made the work under test, where that is known: {"by","model","prompt_sha256"} — a digest of the prompt, never the prompt
verifierwhat checked it: {"id","version","program"}
materialsevery input the verifier read besides the subject, each as {"name","digest","entries"}
checkseach check as {"name","passed","detail","failures"}: what it found, and each failure named

verified_atwhen the verifier ran, UTC, RFC 3339

The evidence: predicate.peer_log

PeerLogEvidence is what a peer-log proof rests on.

FieldTypeMeans
peerstringPeer is the feed the peer's log arrives on, by the name it has here.
keysPeerKeysKeys are the keys the peer signs its tree heads with.
atstringAt is the time the log is claimed complete up to, RFC 3339.
heldintHeld is how many of the peer's records are held, in order from its first.
rootstringRoot is the Merkle root this deployment computes over them; empty when none is held.
headsintHeads is how many tree heads the peer signed were kept, one with each batch taken.
agreeingintAgreeing is how many of them still verify with Keys and are the Merkle root of the first so-many records held.
disagreeintDisagree is how many are not, and Disagreeing lists them, up to MaxFailures.
disagreeing[]PeerHead
last*audit.SignedHeadLast is the last head kept, as the peer signed it.
last_receivedstringLastReceived is when the batch that carried it was taken, by this deployment's clock, and LastRecords how many records that batch held.
last_recordsint
full_batchintFullBatch is the most records a batch carries: a last batch smaller than this reached the end of the peer's log.
fork_countintForkCount is how many heads the peer signed and sent that are not the log held, and Forks carries them, up to MaxPeerForks.
forks[]PeerFork
forks_recordedintForksRecorded is how many forks from this feed this deployment's own audit log recorded as they arrived. More recorded than kept means evidence of one was removed from the feed's files afterwards.

Generated from the program's own definition of this predicate, so these are the names a proof carries.

Verifying one

aertun proof verify --key proof-key.json PROOF.json                 # both signatures, and the verdict
python3 scripts/verifyproof/verify.py PROOF.json proof-key.json     # without Aertun

Both signatures are over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding of the payload: Ed25519 as DSSE defines it, and ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) in the context aertun/proof/v1. A signature says which key signed, never whose; whether to trust the key is yours to decide, and a store's trusted signers say whose it is there. Nothing an author wrote is copied into a proof beyond identifiers and numbers that passed a strict parser.
