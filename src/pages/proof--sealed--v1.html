A proof of this kind is an in-toto Statement v1 in a DSSE envelope whose predicateType is this page's address. Every kind is described on Proofs.

What it claims

An alert, by the SHA-256 of its RFC 8785 canonical form, was sealed in an entry of Aertun's audit log, the log verifies from its first entry, and a Bitcoin timestamp attests a head of the log that covers that entry: the alert existed no later than the block that holds it.

Its verdict

refuted when the log stops verifying or was rewritten, or the batch kept for the seal does not reach the root its entry committed; proven when the alert is in a sealed batch, the batch is in the log, and a head covering it is anchored in a Bitcoin block whose header was checked; inconclusive until then. The alert itself is never in the proof.

aertun alert seal alerts.jsonl
aertun auditlog anchor
aertun proof sealed --alert alert.json --key signing.key --out sealed.proof.json

What every proof carries

FieldHolds

The evidence: predicate.sealed

SealedEvidence is what a sealed-alert proof rests on.

FieldTypeMeans
digeststringDigest is the alert's identity: the SHA-256, in hex, of its JSON in the canonical form of RFC 8785, and the subject's digest. The alert itself is not carried.
log_entriesintLogEntries is how many entries the audit log held when it was read, counting one that could not be read.
log_breakintLogBreak is the first entry, counting from one, that did not re-hash, did not link to the one before it, was out of sequence or could not be read; zero when every entry verified.
rewrittenintRewritten is the size of a head the log sent to be anchored that its first entries no longer hash to — the log rewritten after the head left the machine; zero when every such head still matches.
batchesintBatches is how many batches the log records sealing: the batches the digest was looked for in.
unkeptintUnkept is how many of those batches have no readable record of their digests here, so the digest could not be looked for in them.
entryintEntry is the audit entry that committed the earliest batch holding the digest; zero when none did.
sealedstringSealed is when that entry was written, as the log recorded it, RFC 3339 to the nanosecond: the log's own clock, which an anchor bounds.
batch_rootstringBatchRoot is the RFC 6962 Merkle root over the batch's digests, as the entry committed it.
batch_sizeintBatchSize is how many digests the entry says the batch holds.
batch_indexintBatchIndex is the digest's place in the batch, counting from zero.
batch_path[]stringBatchPath is the RFC 6962 audit path from the digest's leaf to the batch root, nearest the leaf first.
entry_leafstringEntryLeaf is the entry's leaf in the audit log's Merkle tree, which internal/audit derives from the entry's content and its chain hashes.
head_sizeintHeadSize is how many entries the head the entry is shown in covers: the anchored head when one covers the entry, otherwise the log as read.
head_rootstringHeadRoot is that head's Merkle root: for an anchored head, the value the timestamp commits to.
head_path[]stringHeadPath is the RFC 6962 audit path from the entry's leaf, at index Entry-1, to the head's root, nearest the leaf first.
anchorstringAnchor is what fixes that head in time: bitcoin for an attestation whose block was checked, unconfirmed for one whose block was not, pending for a head a calendar holds and has not yet put in a block, none when no head sent to be anchored covers the entry.
heightintHeight is the Bitcoin block the attestation names, when Anchor is bitcoin or unconfirmed.
block_hashstringBlockHash is that block's hash, when Anchor is bitcoin: what a reader looks up at that height, in any node or explorer, to see the block is in the chain.
minedstringMined is the time the block's header carries, RFC 3339, when Anchor is bitcoin. Bitcoin lets a block's own time run up to two hours off.
headerstringHeader is the block's header, eighty bytes in hex, when Anchor is bitcoin. Its hash is BlockHash and its time is Mined; the work it shows is checked again whenever the proof is; and its merkle root is the value the OpenTimestamps proof reaches from HeadRoot, which the command checked before signing.

Generated from the program's own definition of this predicate, so these are the names a proof carries.

Verifying one

aertun proof verify --key proof-key.json PROOF.json                 # both signatures, and the verdict
python3 scripts/verifyproof/verify.py PROOF.json proof-key.json     # without Aertun

Both signatures are over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding of the payload: Ed25519 as DSSE defines it, and ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) in the context aertun/proof/v1. A signature says which key signed, never whose; whether to trust the key is yours to decide, and a store's trusted signers say whose it is there. Nothing an author wrote is copied into a proof beyond identifiers and numbers that passed a strict parser.
