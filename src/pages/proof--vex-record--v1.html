A proof of this kind is an in-toto Statement v1 in a DSSE envelope whose predicateType is this page's address. Every kind is described on Proofs.

What it claims

Over a period, the not_affected statements one party issued were contradicted by the evidence at most a declared share of the time, counted over the not-affected proofs kept here about that party and signed by a key this store trusts.

Its verdict

refuted when the exact lower bound on the contradicted share is over the tolerance, naming the contradicting proofs by id; proven when the upper bound is at or under it on at least 30 checked statements; inconclusive otherwise. Statements that could not be checked are counted and never in the rate, and a proof signed by a key nobody trusted is left out and counted.

aertun vuln vex-record --source NAME --since 2026-10-01 --tolerance 0.05 --key signing.key --out record.proof.json

What every proof carries

FieldHolds
verdictproven, refuted or inconclusive
claimthe sentence that was checked, in words
authorwho made the work under test, where that is known: {"by","model","prompt_sha256"} — a digest of the prompt, never the prompt

The evidence: predicate.vex_record

VEXRecordEvidence is what a VEX record rests on: the proofs it read, the statements they came to, and the contradicted share with its bounds.

FieldTypeMeans
sourcestringSource is the operator's short name for the issuer, as the not-affected proofs read name it.
sincestringSince is the start of the period the proofs were made in, by the time each says its verifier ran, RFC 3339 in UTC.
untilstringUntil is the end of the period, not included.
tolerancefloat64Tolerance is the contradicted share the issuer is held to, declared before the record was made.
readintRead is every kept proof the record read: the not-affected proofs about the issuer made in the period, and every kept proof that no longer verifies. It is LeftOut, UntrustedLeftOut, Superseded and Used together.
left_outintLeftOut is the kept proofs that no longer verify, counted and never used. Every one in the store is counted, because what a proof that does not verify says it is about cannot be believed.
untrusted_left_outintUntrustedLeftOut is the proofs left out because a key this store does not trust signed them, counted and never used; zero when they were asked for.
include_untrustedboolIncludeUntrusted says proofs signed by keys this store does not trust were asked for and used.
supersededintSuperseded is the proofs of a document about an asset that a later proof of the same document about the same asset replaced, so no statement is counted twice.
usedintUsed is the proofs the counts below are taken from.
signed_by_trustedintTrusted is how many of the proofs used were signed by a key this store trusts (internal/proofstore's signers).
signed_by_untrustedintUntrusted is how many of the proofs used were signed by a key this store does not trust: anybody can make a key that signs a proof.
assetsintAssets is how many assets the proofs used held documents against.
statementsintStatements is every statement the documents made, whatever its status.
not_affectedintNotAffected is the not_affected statements among them, each of which was tested.
supportedintSupported is the tested statements the evidence supported, one for each package a statement is about.
contradictedintContradicted is the tested statements the evidence contradicted, one for each package a statement is about.
uncheckableintUncheckable is the tested statements nothing here could decide, one for each package a statement is about: counted, and never in the rate.
contradicting_proofsintContradictingProofs is how many of the proofs used found at least one statement contradicted: the proofs a refutation names.
contradicted_shareBound — k of n with exact one-sided bounds at 95% each: {"k","n","lower","upper"}Share is the contradicted statements of the checked ones, supported or contradicted, with its exact one-sided bounds: the rate the verdict reads. Uncheckable statements are never in it.
min_checkedintMinChecked is the fewest checked statements a proven verdict may rest on.

Generated from the program's own definition of this predicate, so these are the names a proof carries.

Verifying one

aertun proof verify --key proof-key.json PROOF.json                 # both signatures, and the verdict
python3 scripts/verifyproof/verify.py PROOF.json proof-key.json     # without Aertun

Both signatures are over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding of the payload: Ed25519 as DSSE defines it, and ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) in the context aertun/proof/v1. A signature says which key signed, never whose; whether to trust the key is yours to decide, and a store's trusted signers say whose it is there. Nothing an author wrote is copied into a proof beyond identifiers and numbers that passed a strict parser.
