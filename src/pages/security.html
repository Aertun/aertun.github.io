What it may connect to

aertun network

Every outbound connection is for one declared purpose: an action, anchoring, a connector, mail, the AI analyst's model, the SIEM stream, shared signals, Slack reminders, single sign-on and notice webhooks. An undeclared purpose is refused in every mode, and network.mode offline refuses everything that would leave the host. A destination that should be on the internet — a connector, an identity provider, a webhook — is refused at a private address unless a range is declared for it, as aertun oidc configure --reach does for a provider run in-house; a model with no key, the other way round, must be on this machine's network. Nothing reaches link-local, where cloud metadata lives.

Sealed credentials

aertun vault status
aertun vault rotate

A connector's credentials and a feed's secret are sealed under a keyring, so the store's files alone give nobody the values: a lost laptop, a backup on an open bucket, a disposed disk. With no keyring, none can be stored.

Sign-in

Everybody signs in with two factors: a passkey that checks it is them, or single sign-on that says it used two. Failed sign-ins lock out places, not people, and sessions end after an hour unused, fifteen minutes for anybody who can grant access.

The AI only suggests

The AI analyst and agents over MCP read and propose. Neither can record a decision, approve an action, change who may do what, sign or keep a proof, or reach another platform. Every agent is registered before it acts and can be stopped in one act. See Findings, AI agents and AI agents over MCP.

Proofs anybody can check

A proof is signed with Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65 over DSSE's pre-authentication encoding, and checks without Aertun: scripts/verifyproof/verify.py uses Python's cryptography package and OpenSSL. A proof counts as this organisation's only when its key is a trusted signer, which takes two administrators. See Whose proof it is.
